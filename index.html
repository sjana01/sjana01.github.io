Research

My research combines pure mathematics, quantum information, and computational methods, with a focus on structure, classification, and scalable inference.

Quantum Computing

As a postdoctoral researcher, I study quantum nonlocality, contextuality, and magic state distillation through algebraic, graph-theoretic, and computational techniques.

  • Developed graph-based frameworks for modeling quantum nonlocality as constraint satisfaction problems.
  • Classified three-qubit nonlocality paradoxes beyond the GHZ scenario, identifying new families of paradoxes and their algebraic structure.
  • Developed invariant-theoretic methods for magic state distillation, scaling analysis to codes up to 71 qubits / 41 qutrits.

Algebraic Topology

My PhD research focused on algebraic topology, especially cohomological methods related to unordered flag manifolds and symmetric group actions. This work develops structural tools for understanding cohomology rings of unordered flag manifolds and related algebraic invariants. For more on this research, see my PhD Thesis.

  • Cohomology of complete unordered flag manifolds.
  • Mod-2 cohomology rings of alternating subgroups of Coxeter groups of Type B.
  • Connections between group cohomology, topology, and combinatorial algebra.

LLM Hallucination Detector

Erdős Institute Data Science Boot Camp · Spring 2026

    We built a hallucination detection framework for Transformer-based generative models using Python, PyTorch, and Scikit-Learn. The project combined white-box and black-box signals by extracting spectral features from attention-head Laplacians and geometric features from sampled response clouds. Across 2,500 queries and five benchmark datasets, the framework achieved 0.91 AUROC and served as a lightweight pre-validation layer for identifying risky LLM outputs before downstream verification.
Transformers PyTorch LLMs Model Evaluation Scikit-Learn

Tensorized CNNs for Amazon Deforestation Analysis

Multiverse Computing via PIMS M2PI · Summer 2023

    We optimized a deep learning pipeline for Amazon deforestation analysis by replacing ResNet50 with Tensorized CNN architectures, reducing the model size by approximately 90%. This approach achieved roughly a 10x reduction in FLOPs while maintaining comparable predictive performance on high-dimensional image data. The project involved analyzing around 5,000 images using TensorFlow/Keras and reduced training time by approximately 40%.
